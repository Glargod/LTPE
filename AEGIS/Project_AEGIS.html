1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Project AEGIS delivers a dual-platform (air + ground) autonomous robotic system optimized for Canadian Armed Forces operations in contested, GPS-denied, and extreme cold environments. At its core is the Line-of-Sight Priority-Guided Escape (LTPE) v6.3 navigation kernel, which enables resilient autonomous behaviour under full electromagnetic control (EMCON) with near-zero decision latency.

AEGIS combines robust ground platforms with low-bandwidth air-to-ground data seeding to create a resilient Total Force Mesh, significantly improving situational awareness and extraction capability for infantry and medical support units.

2. CAPABILITY GAP ADDRESSED

The Canadian Armed Forces require attritable, sovereign autonomous systems that can operate reliably in high-threat, communications-denied, and Arctic environments (-40°C and below). Many existing solutions depend on continuous GPS, high-bandwidth links, or centralized control — creating vulnerabilities in contested scenarios. AEGIS addresses this through local line-of-sight decision-making and tactical mesh networking.

3. ARCHITECTURAL PILLARS

  • The Brain – LTPE v6.3 Navigation Kernel: A reactive, survival-oriented system using line-of-sight greedy planning, dead-end pruning, and a lightweight entropy-resolution layer to maintain performance in high-chaos, partially observable environments.
  • The Body – Distributed Sovereignty: Zonal 800V DC power architecture with Lithium Titanate (LTO) batteries housed in shielded compartments, hardened for electromagnetic resilience and Arctic conditions.
  • The Intelligence – Total Force Mesh: Low-bandwidth tactical mesh where designated air swarm LoS Hubs seed compressed situational awareness data (threat mapping, terrain updates, extraction cues) to ground units.

5. VALIDATION – MONTE CARLO SIMULATION OF SIMULATION (SoS)

Performance was evaluated using a Monte Carlo Simulation of Simulation (SoS) framework over 1,000 trials per scenario. Each trial models interacting air and ground swarms under realistic uncertainties (40% sensor dropout, dynamic debris, terrain occlusion, and full EMCON). Designated air LoS Hubs provide low-bandwidth data seeding to the ground swarm.
