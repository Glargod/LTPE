1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Project AEGIS delivers a dual-platform (air + ground) autonomous robotic system optimized for Canadian Armed Forces operations in contested and GPS-denied environments, particularly the Arctic. At its core is the Line-of-Sight Priority-Guided Escape (LTPE) navigation kernel, which enables resilient autonomous behaviour under full electromagnetic control (EMCON) with near-zero decision latency.

AEGIS combines robust ground platforms with low-bandwidth air-to-ground data seeding to create a true Total Force Mesh, providing infantry and medical support units with enhanced situational awareness and extraction capability even when traditional communications are denied.

2. CAPABILITY GAP ADDRESSED

The Canadian Armed Forces require attritable, sovereign autonomous systems capable of operating in high-threat, communications-denied, and extreme cold environments (-40°C and below). Current commercial solutions often depend on continuous GPS, high-bandwidth links, or centralized control, creating single points of failure in contested Arctic or forward operating scenarios. AEGIS directly addresses this by prioritizing local line-of-sight decision-making and resilient mesh networking.

3. ARCHITECTURAL PILLARS

  • The Brain – LTPE v6.3 Navigation Kernel: A reactive, survival-oriented navigation system that uses line-of-sight greedy planning, dead-end pruning, and a lightweight “Ritual Layer” (a logarithmic entropy-resolution mechanism) to maintain reliable performance in high-chaos, partially observable environments.
  • The Body – Distributed Sovereignty: Zonal 800V DC power architecture with Lithium Titanate (LTO) batteries housed in shielded “Dead Space” compartments for electromagnetic hardening and Arctic-rated operation.
  • The Intelligence – Total Force Mesh: Low-bandwidth tactical mesh networking where designated air swarm LoS Hubs seed compressed situational awareness data (threat mapping, terrain updates, extraction cues) to ground units, enabling coordinated action without reliance on global maps or high-power transmissions.

5. VALIDATION – MONTE CARLO SYSTEM-OF-SYSTEMS (SoS) TESTING

Performance was evaluated using a Monte Carlo SoS framework (1,000 trials per scenario) that explicitly models interacting air and ground swarms. Each trial incorporates realistic uncertainties including 40% sensor dropout, dynamic debris/obstacles, variable terrain occlusion, and full EMCON conditions. The air swarm provides elevated LoS Hub seeding via low-bandwidth mesh to the ground swarm.
