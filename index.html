LTPE

Line-of-Sight Teleport Priority-Guided Escape
A sparse, goal-directed exploration algorithm for confined organic spaces

Executive Summary

In unknown, confined 3D environments — caves, mines, asteroid lava tubes, collapsed structures — the critical goal is often escape to safety (surface, fresh air, refuge) rather than exhaustive mapping or shortest-path optimality.

LTPE is a lightweight, ant-inspired method that reaches goals with very low step count by:

  • Scanning from hubs to discover visible features
  • Teleporting (or fast-forwarding) to promising points at low cost
  • Pruning dead-ends after cheap confirmation
  • Retroactively boosting priority of paths that lead to rich branches

Simulation on average ant-nest structures: \~148 steps to exit vs. 400–800+ for backtracking/exhaustive methods.

Problem & Motivation

Target Environments

  • Cave search & rescue (SAR)
  • Underground mining accidents
  • Asteroid / lunar / Martian subsurface voids
  • Collapsed buildings or industrial confined spaces

Key Challenges

  • Irregular, organic geometry (curves, chambers, shafts)
  • Limited visibility (dust, darkness, short LOS)
  • Resource constraints (battery, air, compute, time)
  • Frequent dead-ends that punish backtracking
  • Goal: any safe path to exit > shortest path > full map

Biological Inspiration

Fire ant and leafcutter ant nests (visualized via aluminum casts) show:

  • Central queen chamber as high-connectivity hub
  • Radiating tunnels with curves, branches, many short dead-end stubs
  • Scouts prioritize promising cues (airflow, depth, traffic)
  • Dead-ends rejected quickly after brief check
  • Rich downstream discoveries reinforce incoming paths

These structures are spongy, irregular, and chamber-heavy — ideal for sparse LOS exploration.

LTPE Algorithm

Data Structures

  • Nodes: scan points (chambers, curve stops, junctions) with (x,y,z)
  • Exif entries: [az, el, dist, pri (0–15), status, origin_node_id]
  • Queue: FIFO of exif entries + occasional pri-greedy override

Actions & Costs (sight-only)

  • Scan(current node): −1 step per visible direction
  • Teleport to exif entry: +1 step (LOS jump, curvature ignored)
  • Arrival → mandatory scan (even dead-end)

Dead-End Pruning

Scan shows only reversal → scratch that exif entry

Priority (0–15)

  • Initial: elevation (down +), width estimate, airflow proxy, etc.
  • Boost: k ≥ 4 new directions at arrival → +floor(k/2) to origin path pri (cap 15)
  • Selection: mostly FIFO; greedy pull if pri delta > threshold

Optional Variant: Explore / Map Mode

Explore/Map Mode disables aggressive pruning and goal bias to produce a more complete map:

  • Do not scratch dead-end entries → mark status = "dead-end"
  • Keep all nodes and connections
  • Lower pri threshold for exploration (force visit more branches)
  • Optionally disable pri-greedy overrides → pure FIFO or breadth-first
  • After queue exhaustion: export graph (nodes + edges) for 3D reconstruction
  • Use case: scientific mapping, post-incident analysis, infrastructure planning

Trade-off: 2–4× more steps, but yields near-complete topology instead of escape path.

Simulation Results

Average modeled fire ant nest (\~60 segments, \~20 chambers, 90–120 cm depth):

  • Single-agent LTPE: \~148 net steps to surface exit
  • \~85 teleports (+1 each)
  • \~63 scans (net after directions discovered)
  • Peak queue \~21; \~24 nodes kept
  • Classic DFS backtracking: 400–800+ steps
  • BFS/flood: 300–600+ steps

Applications

  • Cave SAR: handheld LiDAR device, voice/AR cues
  • Mining: helmet integration with gas sensors for pri boost
  • Space / asteroid: rover/astronaut in lava tubes, depth-to-surface proxy

Advantages & Limitations

AdvantagesLimitations
  • Very low visited fraction (\~20–50%)
  • Cheap dead-end rejection
  • Adaptive bias without heavy computation
  • Deterministic & explainable
  • No shortest-path guarantee
  • Pri bias risk of local optima
  • Requires decent LOS sensing
